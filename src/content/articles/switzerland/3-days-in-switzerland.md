Switzerland is the most beautiful country I've hiked in and the most expensive place I've travelled, and there's no way to write an honest itinerary without dealing with both of those facts.

So let's start with the money, because it's the thing that actually determines whether this trip is possible for you.

---

## What three days cost me: around €600

**That's €600 for three days, all in** — accommodation, transport, gondolas, everything.

And here's the part that matters: **I cooked every single meal in the hostel kitchen.** If you eat out, it will be meaningfully more than this. Switzerland is a country where a mediocre restaurant meal costs what a good one costs elsewhere in Europe, and the supermarket-and-hostel-kitchen approach is genuinely the difference between this trip being affordable and not.

That's not a complaint, it's just the reality. Plan for it and you'll be fine. Turn up expecting Germany prices and you'll have a miserable time.

### The Swiss Travel Pass

**I didn't buy it, and for three days that was the right call.** But it's close, and **if you're staying longer it's very likely worth it** — the maths tips in its favour the more days and the more mountain transport you're doing.

Work it out against your actual planned journeys rather than buying it reflexively. For a short, geographically tight trip like this one, individual tickets won.

---

## Where to base yourself

**I stayed in Lauterbrunnen**, and I'd recommend it — you wake up in a valley with cliffs on both sides and waterfalls coming off them.

But this is genuinely a free choice: **Lauterbrunnen, Interlaken and Grindelwald all work**, because **the trains between all three are extremely easy.** This is Switzerland; the transport is the one thing that will never be the problem. Pick whichever has the accommodation you want at the price you want and don't overthink it.

---

## Day 1: Lauterbrunnen and Interlaken

**Lauterbrunnen valley.** Start here. The valley has cliffs on both sides and waterfalls pouring off them, and the walk to **Staubbach Falls** is short and worth doing straight away.

**Interlaken in the afternoon.** A short train ride, a lakeside walk, and mountain views in most directions. Interlaken is more of a base town than a destination, but the setting does the work.

## Day 2: Bachalpsee from First — and the best descent in the Alps

This was the most fun day of the trip, and the descent is the part nobody tells you about.

### The hike to Bachalpsee

From **Grindelwald**, take the gondola up to **First**.

**From the top gondola station, the hike to Bachalpsee is very easy and takes about an hour.** That's it. No scrambling, no exposure, no real elevation to speak of — an hour of straightforward walking to a mountain lake that reflects the peaks behind it.

It's the best views-to-effort ratio I found in Switzerland, and it's completely accessible to anyone who can walk for an hour.

![Bachalpsee](/images/switzerland/articles/bachalpsee.jpg)

### The descent: mountain cart, then bike

**This is my single best tip for the Bernese Oberland.**

Don't just ride the gondola back down. Instead:

1. **Walk down from the top to one station below the summit stop.**
2. **Take the mountain cart down to the next station.** These are low go-kart-style buggies you steer down a gravel service track. It is enormous fun.
3. **Then take the bike down to the bottom** for the final section.

Doing the descent in those three stages was **a really amazing experience** and one of the highlights of the whole trip. It costs more than just riding the gondola down, and it's the one upsell in Switzerland I'd tell you to actually pay for.

## Day 3: Oeschinensee — and which trail to take

**The hike around Oeschinensee was the best memory of my entire trip, and it's possibly the most beautiful lake I have ever seen.**

It's also the day where picking the right trail matters most, because there are two routes and they are not equivalent.

### Take this route

From the gondola station, **head straight towards the lake.** Once you're at the lake, **go left from the restaurant** — there's a trail that climbs up and around to the far end of the lake, where there's a hut at the top.

**Take that one.** It has real elevation gain, but it's easy walking, there are **no significant exposed sections**, and here's the key thing: **the views are the same as the harder route.** You are not trading scenery for safety.

### Not this one

There's a second, harder trail that **starts before you reach the lake**, branching off the direct route from the gondola station.

**It has a lot of exposed sections.** I started it and **turned back halfway**, and I don't regret that at all. If you're not genuinely comfortable with exposure, don't begin it — especially since, as above, it doesn't reward you with anything the easy route doesn't already give you.

That's the whole decision: same views, much less exposure, one obvious choice.

## On Jungfraujoch: I skipped it

The big question everyone asks about this region, so here's my honest position.

**I didn't do Jungfraujoch, purely because of the price.** It looks amazing and I'm not disputing that — but on a trip where I was cooking every meal in a hostel to keep the budget under control, it wasn't defensible.

**My plan is to do it once in my life, with my family**, because they can't hike and it's the way for them to experience high alpine scenery without one. That's what it's genuinely good for.

If you *can* hike, my honest view is that the money is better spent on the gondolas, the trails and the mountain cart descent above. You'll see comparable scenery and do more.

---

## Worth knowing

- **Cook your own food.** This is the single biggest lever on the cost of a Swiss trip.
- **Layers.** Mountain weather turns fast, and it's much colder at the top of a gondola than in the valley.
- **Book gondolas and mountain transport ahead in summer**, particularly at First and Oeschinensee.
- **Work out the Swiss Travel Pass against your actual route** rather than assuming either way.
- **Three days is enough for the Bernese Oberland**, but only for the Bernese Oberland. Don't try to add Zurich or Zermatt onto this.
