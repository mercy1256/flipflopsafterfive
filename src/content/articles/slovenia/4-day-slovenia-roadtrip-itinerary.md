The best thing we did in Slovenia was two hours underground at Postojna. The second best was getting into the Soča River. Neither of those is Lake Bled, which is the picture everyone comes for — and which was, honestly, the weakest part of the trip.

We did this over a long weekend in August, driving down from Germany with friends. The drive ate a full day each way, but it's the reason the trip works at all: almost everything worth seeing in Slovenia is off a road somewhere, and the country is small enough that you're never more than a couple of hours from the next thing.

Four days on the ground, two nights in Ljubljana and two in Bled.

---

## Day 1: The drive, and Ljubljana after dark

A full day behind the wheel, arriving in Ljubljana in the evening. That sounds like a wasted day and it isn't, because the old town is genuinely better at night.

The streets along the Ljubljanica are lit, the cafés spill outside, there's usually music somewhere, and the whole thing stays relaxed rather than rowdy. After a day in the car it was exactly the right speed. Eat there, walk the river, don't plan anything else.

![Ljubljana Old Town](/images/slovenia/articles/ljubljana-old-town.jpg)

One practical thing before you cross the border: Slovenian motorways require a vignette, and you want it sorted before you're on them.

---

## Day 2: Ljubljana Castle, Postojna, Predjama, and one town too many

Ljubljana Castle in the morning — funicular up, or walk it if you'd rather. It's a decent hour or two and good views over the city, but it isn't why you came.

**Postojna is why you came.** The tour is about two hours, and it's the one thing on this trip I'd tell anyone to do without qualification. It cost us around €45 each and we bought the tickets on the spot on the day — I'd expected to need to book weeks ahead and we simply didn't, and it was far less crowded inside than I'd braced for. Part of the tour is on a train that runs deep into the system before you walk the rest, which is what lets it cover the distance it does. Two hours underground sounds like a lot. It goes quickly.

![Postojna Caves](/images/slovenia/articles/postojna-caves.jpg)

**Predjama Castle** is a short drive on, and I'll be straight about it: the outside is the whole thing. A castle built into the mouth of a cave is a genuinely startling sight and worth the stop to stand in front of. We went inside as well, and it wasn't that impressive — medieval rooms much like medieval rooms anywhere. Look at it, take the photo, don't feel obliged to buy the interior ticket.

![Predjama Castle](/images/slovenia/articles/predjama-castle.jpg)

Then we drove to **Piran**, on the coast, and this is the one decision I'd change. Piran itself was fine — a Venetian-looking old town on the Adriatic, narrow alleys, a seafront. Nothing wrong with it. But arriving there at the end of a day that already had a two-hour cave tour in it meant we got a couple of hours and a drive back to Ljubljana in the dark. It wasn't stressful, exactly. It just meant the day had no gaps in it, and Piran is a place you want a slow full day for, not the tail end of one.

Either give Piran its own day or leave it out. Don't staple it onto Postojna.

![Piran](/images/slovenia/articles/piran.jpg)

## Day 3: Vršič Pass and the Soča Valley — the best day of the trip

This is the day the trip is actually about, and the timings matter, so here's exactly how ours ran.

**Lake Jasna in the morning**, at the foot of the pass near Kranjska Gora. Small, clear, mountains behind it, a wooden pier and a bronze ibex statue that everyone photographs. It's a twenty-minute stop, not a destination — but it's the right way to start the day.

![Lake Jasna](/images/slovenia/articles/lake-jasna.jpg)

**The Vršič Pass** from there: fifty hairpin turns over the highest road pass in Slovenia, climbing out of Kranjska Gora and dropping down the other side into the Soča valley. Take it in daylight, take it slowly, and stop — the pull-offs on the way up are the point of the drive, not an interruption to it.

![Vršič Pass](/images/slovenia/articles/vrscic-pass.jpg)

**We reached Velika Korita around 2 PM, and it was by far the best thing on the whole trip.** The Soča is glacier-fed and an almost unreal turquoise, running through narrow rock gorges, and it is the clearest river I've ever swum in. We got in.

Here's the part nobody tells you: **you don't have to go to Velika Korita specifically.** There are swimming spots the entire length of that river. Drive along the valley road, find somewhere to pull over, walk down to the water. That's it. If the marked spot is busy, the next one along won't be.

![Velika Korita Soče](/images/slovenia/articles/velika-korita-soce.jpg)

**Lake Bohinj by about 5 PM** to finish, on the way to Bled. It was the other genuine highlight — bigger, quieter and far less developed than Bled, ringed by mountains, and you can swim here too. If you only have time for one of Slovenia's two famous lakes, I'd take this one.

![Lake Bohinj](/images/slovenia/articles/lake-bohinj.jpg)

That's Jasna in the morning, the pass mid-morning, the Soča from 2 PM and Bohinj by 5 PM. It's a full day and none of it is padding.

## Day 4: Lake Bled and Vintgar Gorge

Lake Bled is beautiful. It is also crowded and expensive, and after the Soča valley it was a slight comedown.

We walked the 6 km loop around the lake, which is the best way to see it and free. We didn't row out to the island and I don't regret it. The island church and the cliff-top castle look their best from the shore anyway, which is where every photo you've ever seen of Bled was taken from.

It's worth a day. It just isn't worth building the trip around, and if you're short on time it's the thing I'd cut before I'd cut the Soča.

![Lake Bled](/images/slovenia/articles/lake-bled.jpg)

**Vintgar Gorge**, a short drive away, is part of Triglav National Park — a boardwalk route pinned to the rock wall above the Radovna River. To be clear, since I've seen it claimed otherwise: **there is no swimming in Vintgar.** It's a walk, and a good one. Save the swimming for the Soča.

![Vintgar Gorge](/images/slovenia/articles/vintgar-gorge.jpg)

## What I'd tell you

- **Go in summer.** We went in August and the swimming is half the reason to come — the lakes and the river are the trip.
- **You need a car.** Every good thing here is a pull-off on a road somewhere.
- **Book nothing except your accommodation.** We walked up and bought Postojna tickets on the day in peak season.
- **Two bases is the right number** — Ljubljana for the caves and the coast, Bled for the alps. We did two nights in each and didn't feel like we were packing and unpacking.
- **If you cut one thing, cut Piran or Bled. Not the Soča valley.**

Slovenia is small enough to feel like you've seen it in four days and varied enough that you haven't. I wouldn't skip anything I did — but I'd give the Soča valley more of the clock and the famous lake less of it.
