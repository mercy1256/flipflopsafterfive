Two of the most photographed places in France in one weekend: the island abbey of **Mont St Michel** and the chalk cliffs and arches at **Etretat**.

I'll say up front which one surprised me. Mont St Michel is the famous one and it's spectacular — but **the cliff walk at Etretat was the better experience**, and it's the part I'd tell anyone not to skip.

---

## Getting there: night bus to Paris, then a hire car

The route that made this work as a weekend:

- **10 PM** — overnight bus out of Germany
- **7 AM** — arrive in Paris
- **8 AM** — pick up the hire car
- **11:30 AM** — arrive at Mont St Michel

The overnight bus isn't comfortable, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But it's cheap and it converts a travel day into a night you were going to spend sleeping anyway. You step off it at 7 AM with the whole day still ahead of you.

**You want the car.** Mont St Michel and Etretat are on opposite sides of Normandy, and the drive out from Paris is roughly three and a half hours. Picking the car up at 8 AM and being at the abbey by 11:30 is the part that makes the timing work at all.

---

## Day 1: Mont St Michel

**I was there from about 11:30 AM until 3 PM**, which turned out to be a good window.

### Parking is easier than you'd expect

This worried me in advance and it shouldn't have. **There are a lot of parking lots**, so finding a space wasn't the problem the forums suggest.

The thing to know is that they're not next to the island. **It's about a 45-minute walk from the parking to Mont St Michel itself.** There's also a **free shuttle bus that does it in around 15 minutes.**

Both are fine. The walk is flat and gives you the abbey growing on the horizon the whole way, which is genuinely worth doing at least one direction. But if you're short on time or the weather turns, the free bus is right there.

### The tides: less of a problem than expected

Mont St Michel's tides are the thing every guide tells you to obsess over, so here's what actually happened: **they weren't much of a hassle.**

**When I arrived the island was fully surrounded by water**, which is the version everyone wants to see — the abbey rising out of the bay with water all around it. I got that without engineering my visit around a tide table.

It's still worth **checking the tide schedule before you go**, because the bay does empty out to sand and the surrounded-by-water look is the more dramatic one. But don't let it dominate your planning the way the guides imply. A late-morning arrival did it for me.

### The abbey was closed — and the town still delivered

Here's my honest experience: **the abbey was closed when I visited, because it was a public holiday.**

Check the opening hours against public holidays before you travel. I didn't, and I lost the main interior.

And yet — **the town itself was amazing.** The narrow cobbled streets winding up the rock, the ramparts, and the views back over the bay were more than enough to justify the trip. If you find yourself in the same position, you have not wasted your day.

That's a useful thing to know in advance: Mont St Michel works even without the abbey ticket.

![Mont St Michel](/images/france/articles/mont-st-michel.jpg)

### Overnight in Caen

I left at 3 PM and **reached Caen around 6 PM**, and stayed the night there.

Caen is the practical call rather than a romantic one: it sits between Mont St Michel and Etretat, so it splits the driving into two manageable halves instead of one long haul. Breaking the trip here is what turns this into a comfortable weekend rather than a punishing one.

---

## Day 2: Etretat — do the cliff walk on both sides

This was the highlight of the weekend.

Etretat sits under white chalk cliffs that the sea has cut into arches and a needle of rock standing offshore. There are **cliff paths on both sides of the beach**, and the correct answer is to walk both.

**Each side took about an hour and a half**, so around three hours for the pair.

**It is breathtaking, and I think everyone should do it.** The paths climb up from the town onto the clifftops and give you the arches from above and the coastline running away in both directions. There's no difficulty to it beyond the initial climb.

Being straight with you: **the cliff walk was essentially the only thing to do there**, and we had the whole day. That's not a criticism — it's a full day of one genuinely outstanding thing, which is a better day than most. But don't arrive expecting a town with a lot going on. Arrive expecting cliffs, and give them the time.

The village itself is pleasant for a seaside lunch and a wander along the pebble beach between the two walks, which is exactly how I'd use the middle of the day.

Then it's back to **Paris in the evening**, drop the car, and onto the night bus to Germany.

## Worth knowing

- **Check the abbey's opening hours against public holidays.** This is the mistake I made.
- **Parking at Mont St Michel is plentiful**, but it's a 45-minute walk from the island — or a free 15-minute shuttle.
- **Check the tide schedule**, but don't build the whole trip around it — a late-morning arrival gave me the island surrounded by water.
- **Allow three hours at Etretat** for both cliff walks.
- **Stay in Caen between the two.** It splits the drive and it's roughly halfway.
- **Night bus out, night bus back, hire car in between.** Two nights on a bus buys you two full days in Normandy without paying for two hotels.
- **Layers.** Normandy weather changes fast and the clifftops are exposed.
