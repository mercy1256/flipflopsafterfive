Eight days: three in Phuket, two in Krabi, and the back end in Bangkok. I did it with a friend, but we travelled the way solo travellers do — hostels the whole way, public transport, nothing pre-booked that didn't have to be.

Here's the honest summary before the day-by-day, because it's the thing I'd want told to me straight:

**Phuket and Bangkok are both overrated.** They're where you sleep and where the boats and flights leave from. **The trip is the days on the water and the day in Krabi**, and if you build the itinerary around the cities instead of around those, you'll come home wondering what the fuss was about.

And if I did it again, I'd change nothing about this route — I'd just add a couple of days on the end and go **north to Chiang Mai.**

---

## Day 1: Phuket Old Town and Patong Beach

**Phuket Old Town** is the better half of this day — Sino-Portuguese shophouses, street art, cafés, and it's walkable.

**Patong Beach** is the other half. The sand and the swimming are fine, and **Bangla Road** at night is the thing Patong is famous for.

I'll be blunt: **both are overrated.** Bangla Road is a neon strip that exists to sell you things, and Patong is a beach with a lot of people on it. Go once, see it, form your own opinion — but don't plan days around it.

Two practical notes from the day: **the Big Buddha was closed when I was there**, so check before you make the trip up. And **have a coconut smoothie** — that one I do mean.

![Patong Beach](/images/thailand/articles/patong-beach.jpg)

## Day 2: Koh Phi Phi and Maya Bay

A full day on the water out of Phuket. **I did it by longtail boat**, which I'd recommend over the speedboats — slower, but you're actually in the scenery rather than being fired through it.

**You can still get into Maya Bay.** There's confusion about this because the bay was closed for years, so here's what it actually is now: **the boats dock round the back at Loh Samah, and it's about a 20-minute walk over to Maya Bay itself.** **Swimming in the bay is not allowed** — you look at it, you don't get in it.

And it's still worth doing. Even standing on the sand rather than swimming, **the bay is beautiful.** Just go with the right expectation so you're not annoyed on the day.

**Pileh Lagoon** is on the same route and is the green-water, sheer-cliff one people picture when they think of Phi Phi.

![Koh Phi Phi](/images/thailand/articles/koh-phi-phi.jpg)
![Maya Bay](/images/thailand/articles/maya-bay.jpg)

Book this the day before. **Every street corner in Phuket has a tour operator**, and it's also on GetYourGuide — there's no reason to arrange it from home.

Back on shore, the **Phuket Sunday Night Market** on Thalang Road is the good version of Phuket: street food, mango sticky rice, and a crowd that isn't there for Bangla Road.

---

## Day 3: Phang Nga Bay — the one to do

Another boat day, and **this is the best one.**

**If you only have time for one boat trip, make it Phang Nga Bay, not Phi Phi.** Phi Phi is the famous one and Phang Nga is the better one — limestone towers standing straight out of flat water, caves, and lagoons you get into rather than look at from a distance.

![Phang Nga Bay](/images/thailand/articles/phang-nga-bay.jpg)

**James Bond Island** is the postcard stop and it's busy, which is fine because it's brief.

![James Bond Island](/images/thailand/articles/james-bond-island.jpg)

**Monkey Bay** and **Hong Island** are the rest of the day — Hong Island is where you actually stop and swim, and it's the better use of your time than queuing for the James Bond photo.

![Hong Island](/images/thailand/articles/hong-island.jpg)

---

## Day 4: Phuket to Krabi, and Railay

Be realistic about this one: **half the day goes on getting to Krabi.** That's the whole reason it's not a bigger day.

With the afternoon, go to **Railay Beach.** It's cut off from the mainland by cliffs so you reach it by boat, and it's the most dramatic beach on this itinerary by a distance — the karst walls come right down to the sand.

![Railay Beach](/images/thailand/articles/railay-beach.jpg)

**Krabi Town** itself is a base rather than a destination. Perfectly pleasant, wooden shop-houses, a walking street market at weekends, and that's about the size of it.

---

## Day 5: Tiger Cave Temple and the Emerald Pool

The best day on land.

### Tiger Cave Temple (Wat Tham Suea)

**1,237 steps to the top, and it is genuinely brutal.** Not a stroll with a view at the end — a hard, hot climb up steep uneven stairs.

**I did climb all of them, and the view from the top is worth it.** That's the honest verdict: it's hard, it's not optional if you want the view, and you should do it early before the heat.

![Tiger Cave Temple](/images/thailand/articles/tiger-cave-temple.jpg)

### Emerald Pool and Blue Pool — know which is which

These get written up as one place constantly and they are two different pools on the same jungle trail, with one important difference:

- **The Emerald Pool (Sa Morakot) is the one you can swim in.** It's a natural warm spring — **the water really is warm**, which is a strange thing to feel in that heat and is half the reason it's memorable.
- **The Blue Pool is the vivid turquoise one further along the trail, and you cannot swim in it.** It's a look-at-it pool.

So: walk to both, swim in the Emerald Pool.

![Emerald Pool](/images/thailand/articles/emerald-blue-pool.jpg)

---

## Day 6: Fly to Bangkok

Short domestic flight, and the evening on **Khao San Road** — backpacker central, street food, bars, and worth seeing once for what it is.

---

## Day 7: Grand Palace, Wat Arun and the river

The one full Bangkok day, and it holds everything you need.

**The Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew** in the morning. Dress code is enforced — shoulders and knees covered, no exceptions, and they will turn you away.

![Grand Palace](/images/thailand/articles/grand-palace.jpg)

**Wat Arun** is straight across the river and the better of the two to look at, especially from the far bank in the late afternoon.

![Wat Arun](/images/thailand/articles/wat-arun.jpg)

Finish with a **night cruise on the Chao Phraya.** It's touristy and it's still the right way to end the trip — Wat Arun lit up from the water is the best Bangkok looks.

![Chao Phraya River Cruise](/images/thailand/articles/chao-phraya-river-cruise.jpg)

---

## Day 8: Departure

Fly home from Suvarnabhumi. If you land on a weekend and have the morning free, **Chatuchak Weekend Market** is the one thing worth squeezing in.

---

## What I'd change

**Nothing about the route — I'd just want two more days, and I'd spend them going north to Chiang Mai.** This itinerary is entirely southern Thailand: islands, limestone and one big city. The north is a different country in feel and it's the obvious gap.

Other than that:

- **Phang Nga Bay over Phi Phi** if you can only do one boat day.
- **A day and a half is enough for Bangkok** unless you specifically like city time. I don't, particularly.
- **Don't over-invest in Phuket.** It's the launch pad, not the destination.

---

## Practical notes

- **Book the boat trips in-country, a day ahead.** Every street in Phuket sells them.
- **Take the longtail over the speedboat** if you get the choice.
- **Temples: shoulders and knees covered, shoes off.**
- **Tuk-tuks and taxis** — agree the fare first or insist on the meter.
- **Do the hard things early.** Tiger Cave Temple in the middle of the day would be miserable.
