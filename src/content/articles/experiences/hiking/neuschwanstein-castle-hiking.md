Neuschwanstein is the most photographed building in Germany and the one everyone pictures when they think of a fairytale castle. It's also, for most visitors, an expensive and heavily managed interior tour that they queue a long time for.

I skipped the inside entirely, and I'd make the same call again.

---

## I didn't go in — and here's why

I'd planned to. What changed my mind was **talking to people who'd just come out**, who consistently said it wasn't worth it.

The interior tour is timed, tightly shepherded, and only covers a portion of the building — the castle was never finished, so there's less to see than the exterior leads you to expect. Ludwig II died before it was completed and only a fraction of the rooms were ever fully fitted out.

That's a judgement call rather than a fact, and if seeing the interior of Neuschwanstein is a lifelong ambition, ignore me and book it well in advance. But if you're on the fence, know that the money and the queueing time buy you less than the outside of the building promises.

**What I did instead was the hike, and that's the part I'd tell people not to miss.**

---

## The hike behind the castle

The trails behind and above Neuschwanstein are the best thing here. You get the castle from angles the coach parties never see, set against the Alps, with the lakes below — and once you're twenty minutes up a trail, the crowds thin out almost completely.

This is the reversal worth understanding about Neuschwanstein: the crowds are concentrated in an extremely small area around the castle entrance and the main viewpoint. Walk beyond that and you have the place largely to yourself.

## The Marienbrücke problem

The **Marienbrücke** is the bridge over the gorge that gives you the classic head-on view of the castle. It's the shot you've seen.

Two things you need to know:

**It was closed when I went, and it closes frequently.** Weather, ice, maintenance, structural work — it shuts often and sometimes for extended periods. If that specific photograph is the reason you're making the trip, **check whether the bridge is open before you travel**, not when you arrive.

**Even when it's open, there's always a huge queue.** It's a narrow bridge and everybody on it wants the same picture from the same spot, so it moves slowly.

Plan for it to be closed and treat it as a bonus if it isn't. The hike gives you good views regardless.

---

## Don't skip the Alpsee

This is my other recommendation, and it's the one most people walk straight past.

The **Alpsee**, the lake below the castle, is genuinely beautiful and barely visited compared to the castle above it. Almost everyone arrives, goes up, comes down, and leaves.

Give it an hour or two. Walk along the shore, sit by the water. It's the calmest part of the whole site and it costs nothing.

![The Alpsee below the castle](/images/experiences/hiking/neuschwanstein-alpsee.jpg)

## How I'd structure the day

1. **Arrive early** — the whole area gets busy fast.
2. **Walk up** rather than taking the shuttle or the horse carriage, if you're able. It's part of the experience.
3. **Take the trails behind and above the castle.** This is the main event.
4. **Marienbrücke if it's open**, factoring in the queue.
5. **Down to the Alpsee** for the rest of the afternoon.

---

## Worth knowing

- **Check the Marienbrücke status before travelling** if the classic view matters to you.
- **Proper shoes.** The trails behind the castle are real paths, not promenades.
- **The castle interior needs booking ahead** if you decide to do it — don't count on same-day tickets.
- **Hohenschwangau**, the yellow castle across the valley, is right there if you want a second one.
