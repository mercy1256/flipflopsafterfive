Santa Claus Village sits just outside Rovaniemi, right on the Arctic Circle, and it's the single most famous thing to do in Finnish Lapland. It's also the kind of place that's easy to over-plan or under-plan. Here's what I'd want to know before going again.

We visited as the opening day of our [10-day Finland winter itinerary](/blog/finland/10-days-finland-itinerary), straight off the overnight train from Helsinki.

---

## Time your visit around Christmas Eve

This is the one thing worth planning around. **In Finland, Christmas is celebrated on 24 December**, and the village was closed on that day when we visited — so we went on the **23rd** instead, the last day it was open before Christmas.

If your trip straddles Christmas, check the current year's opening hours before you build your itinerary around a specific day. Being deep in the polar night on the 23rd also meant the whole place was lit up in near-permanent twilight, which is honestly the best way to see it.

---

## How long you actually need

The advice you'll see online tends to assume you're travelling with children. Two different answers here:

- **With kids:** you can comfortably fill **five to six hours**. There's enough spread across the site to keep them busy, and you won't feel like you're padding.
- **Without kids:** **two to three hours** is plenty. We spent about six including lunch, and while I don't regret it, most of that was taking our time rather than running out of things to see.

---

## Meeting Santa, and whether the photo is worth it

You can meet Santa year-round, and it's genuinely well done — he's professional, speaks multiple languages, and it doesn't feel like a conveyor belt.

You can't take your own photos during the meeting. A copy of the official photo costs **€50**, with a video option available too. That's steep, and plenty of people skip it.

I bought both, and I'd do it again. It's a core memory from the trip and not something you get a second run at — but go in knowing the price so it isn't a surprise at the counter.

---

## The Arctic Circle certificate: skippable

The village straddles the Arctic Circle, and there's a marked line you can cross, with a certificate available to commemorate it.

I didn't buy one, and I don't think it's worth the money. That said, if you're the sort of person who likes a physical souvenir, it's a reasonable one — just don't feel you're missing out by walking past. Crossing the line itself is free, and that's the part worth doing.

---

## Santa's main post office

Worth a stop. You can send a postcard with an official Arctic Circle postmark, and there's an option to have a letter delivered at Christmas rather than immediately — a nice thing to send to someone at home.

It's also one of the warmer places on site, which matters more than you'd think when it's this cold.

---

## Activities on site are expensive — book them elsewhere

The village offers reindeer sledding, husky sledding, and horse rides. They're all perfectly good, but they are **noticeably more expensive here than elsewhere in Lapland**, because you're paying for the location.

We did our [husky and reindeer safaris out of Levi instead](/blog/finland/best-activities-in-lapland), where they were cheaper and, crucially, longer — you get proper time out in the forest rather than a short loop. If you're spending more than a day or two in Lapland, I'd save the safaris for a farm outside Rovaniemi.

---

## Do you need to book ahead?

We booked in advance, but honestly it wasn't necessary. Even a day or two before Christmas, it wasn't crowded enough that you'd be turned away.

Booking ahead is still worth it for anything time-slotted — meeting Santa at a specific hour, or any of the sledding activities — but you don't need to lock in your whole visit weeks out.

---

## Final thoughts

Santa Claus Village is genuinely worth the trip, and it's the natural first or last stop on any Lapland itinerary given how close it is to Rovaniemi's airport and train station. Just be realistic about the time you need, budget for the Santa photo if you want it, and do your safaris somewhere cheaper.
