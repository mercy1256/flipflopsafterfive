Tallinn sits about two hours across the Gulf of Finland from Helsinki, which makes it one of the easiest international day trips in Europe. It has one of the best-preserved medieval old towns anywhere, and you can see the best of it comfortably between breakfast and dinner.

We did it as [Day 9 of our 10-day Finland winter itinerary](/blog/finland/10-days-finland-itinerary), and I'd recommend it to anyone spending more than a couple of days in Helsinki.

---

## The ferry

We used **Tallink**, which cost **€35 one way** — so €70 return. The crossing takes about two hours, and the ferries are large, comfortable, and run frequently throughout the day.

You're crossing between two Schengen countries, so there's no border faff, but bring your passport or ID card anyway.

Book ahead if you're travelling around Christmas and New Year, when the sailings fill up with people doing exactly what you're doing.

---

## The timings that worked for us

Our plan was simple and I wouldn't change it:

- **Out on the 7am sailing**, arriving in Tallinn mid-morning
- **Back on the 5pm sailing**, into Helsinki in the evening

That gives you roughly **six to seven hours on the ground**, which is genuinely enough for the old town at an unhurried pace, including lunch and a museum.

The early start is worth it. Going out later compresses your day badly, especially in winter when the light goes early and the cold puts a natural limit on how long you want to be outside.

---

## What to see

The old town is compact enough to cover on foot, and everything below is within a short walk of everything else:

- **The old town itself** — the main event. Cobbled streets, medieval merchant houses, and a genuinely intact town square.
- **The city walls and towers** — you can walk sections of them, and they give you the best views over the red rooftops.
- **The Christmas market**, if you're visiting in December. Tallinn's is one of the better ones in Europe and sits right in the town square.
- **Fotografiska** — a photography museum, and a good option for an hour indoors when you need to warm up.

---

## Day trip or overnight?

**Day trip.** I'd be fairly firm about this.

The old town is excellent, but it's also compact, and once you've walked it, seen the walls, and done a museum, you've had the best of what Tallinn offers a short-stay visitor. If you've already spent time in other European old towns, you'll work through it faster than you expect.

Staying overnight means finding a second day's worth of things to do in a city that doesn't really have them, unless you're specifically going for the food and bar scene. Better to give Tallinn one well-paced day and spend the extra night back in Helsinki — where, as it happens, the restaurants are excellent.

---

## Final thoughts

For €70 and a couple of early-morning hours, you get a second country and one of the best medieval town centres in Europe. It's the single highest-value day in the southern half of a Finland trip.
