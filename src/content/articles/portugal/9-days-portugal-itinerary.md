Nine days down mainland Portugal, from Porto to the Algarve. **I did this trip solo and entirely on public transport** — no hire car at any point — and that turned out to be the easy way to do it rather than the compromise it sounds like.

If you're travelling with a car, the route below works unchanged. But if you're not, this is the part most Portugal itineraries won't tell you: **you genuinely don't need one.**

---

## How I got around

The shape of the trip is an **open-jaw flight — into Porto, out of Faro** — so you never backtrack.

| Leg | How |
| --- | --- |
| Into Porto | Flight |
| Porto → Douro Valley (Pinhão) | Local train |
| Porto → Lisbon | Flixbus |
| Lisbon → Sintra | Train, then bus |
| Lisbon → Cabo da Roca | Train to Cascais, then bus |
| Lisbon → Faro | Flixbus (~3.5 hrs) |
| Faro → Lagos | Local train |
| Out of Faro | Flight |

**I booked almost nothing in advance.** The only thing I reserved ahead was **entry to Pena Palace** in Sintra. Everything else — buses, local trains, boat trips — I sorted on the day.

That's worth knowing, because a lot of Portugal advice pushes you into booking a full itinerary up front. Outside Pena, I never needed to.

---

## Day 1: Porto

**Clerigos Tower.** The city's main landmark, a baroque tower attached to a beautiful church. There's a small fee to climb it and it's worth paying — the view over the city from the top is the best orientation you'll get on day one.

**Livraria Lello.** As a Potterhead this was non-negotiable for me. There's an entry fee, but **if you buy a book inside, the fee is refunded** — so if you were going to buy anything at all, you effectively get in free.

**Jardins do Palácio Cristal.** A stunning park with views over the Douro, and peacocks wandering around it.

**Dom Luis I Bridge.** The main attraction, and the reason to time your day around it: **I watched the best sunset of my life from here.** If you do one thing in Porto, be on this bridge at golden hour.

**São Bento Station.** One of the most beautiful train stations you'll ever walk into, entirely because of the tile work. You'll pass through it anyway on the way to the Douro.

## Day 2: Douro Valley

A day trip out to **Pinhão**, a small town in the heart of the valley, on the **local train from Porto**. **The train ride alone justifies the day** — it runs along the river for the last stretch and it's one of the more scenic rail journeys in Europe.

**The viewpoint hike.** About an hour on foot gets you a panoramic view over the terraced valley.

**Boat ride and wine tasting.** A slow trip on the river and a tasting in the oldest demarcated wine region in the world.

![Douro Valley](/images/portugal/articles/douro.jpg)

## Day 3: Lisbon

Porto to Lisbon on the **Flixbus**, then straight into the city.

**Belém.** Jerónimos Monastery, the Torre de Belém, the waterfront, and the pastries the neighbourhood is famous for.

**Praça do Comércio.** The main square, and the best part of the city for simply walking. **Pink Street** is a short detour and worth it.

**Miradouro de São Pedro de Alcântara.** One of the best sunset viewpoints in Lisbon.

![Belém](/images/portugal/articles/belém.jpg)
![Lisbon](/images/portugal/articles/lisbon.jpg)

## Day 4: Sintra

**Train from Lisbon, then a bus up the hill.** Straightforward, and no car required despite what you'll read.

**Pena Palace.** Iconic, absurdly colourful, and **the one thing on this whole trip I booked in advance.** Do the same — it's the single busiest attraction on the route.

![Pena Palace](/images/portugal/articles/pena.jpg)

**Quinta da Regaleira — my favourite thing in Sintra.** Vast, strange and full of detail, and if you're choosing between Sintra's palaces on limited time, choose this one over the others.

**National Palace of Sintra.** Close to Pena and easy to add on, but honestly an average experience next to the other two. If your day is running short, this is what to drop.

![Quinta da Regaleira](/images/portugal/articles/regaleira.jpg)

---

## Day 5: Cabo da Roca and the coast

**Train from Lisbon to Cascais, then a bus out to the cape.** Again — no car.

**Cabo da Roca.** The westernmost point of continental Europe, and the scenery does live up to it.

![Cabo Da Roca](/images/portugal/articles/cabo.jpg)

**Praia da Ursa.** A genuinely hidden beach, and the catch is the access: **it's about an hour of cliff descent to get down.** Budget for the fact that you then have to climb back up. Go if you've got the legs and the time; skip it if you're tired or the light is going.

![Praia da Ursa](/images/portugal/articles/ursa.jpg)

**Boca do Inferno.** An ocean-carved rock formation right by Cascais, so it fits neatly on the way back to the train.

---

## Day 6: The travel day to Faro

Being straight with you: **this day is the Flixbus.**

The ride from Lisbon to Faro is around three and a half hours, but between getting to the bus, the journey itself, and getting into town at the other end, **it consumed the day.** I got to the beach in the evening and that was it.

I'd rather flag that than dress it up. On a nine-day trip run entirely on public transport, one day gets eaten by the length of the country — plan it as a transfer day, don't schedule anything into it, and enjoy the beach when you land.

## Day 7: Lagos

**Local train from Faro to Lagos.**

**Benagil Cave, by boat.** The cave is only reachable from the water, and the standard way in is a boat tour out of Lagos. It's iconic and it is **very** crowded.

![Benagil Cave](/images/portugal/articles/benagil.jpg)

**Praia do Camilo.** A beautiful beach under dramatic cliffs.

**Ponta da Piedade.** More sea caves and rock arches, and the thing not to skip in Lagos.

![Ponta da Piedade](/images/portugal/articles/piedade.jpg)

---

## Day 8: The Seven Hanging Valleys, and Benagil properly

**The trail** runs from Praia do Vale de Centeanes to Praia da Marinha — around three hours along the clifftops, and the best walking on the route.

![Seven Hanging Valleys](/images/portugal/articles/seven-hanging-valleys.jpg)

### Do Benagil by kayak, not by boat

I ended up seeing Benagil **three ways in two days** — by boat tour from Lagos, by kayak, and from the viewpoint above it on the trail. So this is a comparison rather than a guess:

**Go by kayak.** The boat tours pull in, everyone photographs the same hole in the roof, and they pull straight back out. **With a kayak there's a small beach inside the cave, and you can stay.** That's the whole difference — the boats come in waves, and **when they clear out you get a few minutes of the cave essentially to yourself.** That's the version people are picturing when they book the boat.

It is crowded either way. But the kayak is what buys you the quiet moments.

**Praia da Marinha.** One of the best beaches in Europe, and exactly where you want the trail to finish.

![Praia da Marinha](/images/portugal/articles/marinha.jpg)

---

## Day 9: Faro, briefly

My flight was at 12:30, so this was breakfast and the airport. **Treat day 9 as a departure morning, not a sightseeing day** — if you want time in Faro itself, take it on the evening you arrive rather than counting on the last morning.

---

## Worth knowing

- **You do not need a car.** Flixbus for the two long legs, local trains for everything else.
- **Fly into Porto and out of Faro.** An open-jaw ticket means no backtracking.
- **Book Pena Palace ahead.** It was the only thing on nine days that needed it.
- **Benagil by kayak beats Benagil by boat**, because you can stay inside the cave.
- **Lose a day to the Lisbon–Faro bus** and plan around it rather than fighting it.
- **Livraria Lello refunds the entry fee** against a book.
- **Sunset from the Dom Luis I Bridge** is the one thing I'd tell you not to miss in Porto.
