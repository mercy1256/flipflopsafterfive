Montserrat is a mountain of strange, rounded rock pillars about 50 minutes from Barcelona, with a monastery built directly into its side and a full day of hiking above it. If you have a spare day in Barcelona, this is the one I'd spend it on.

It's also the day trip people most commonly get wrong on the way out, because there are two different ways up the mountain and they leave from **two different train stations**. Getting that one detail right is most of what this guide is for.

---

## Getting there: the bit everyone gets wrong

There's a direct local train from **Plaça d'Espanya** in Barcelona. That part is easy. What's less obvious is that you choose your way up the mountain *when you buy your ticket*, and that choice determines where you get off.

**Buy a combined return ticket** covering the train from Barcelona plus your ascent. Then:

- **Cable car (Aeri de Montserrat)** — get off at **Aeri de Montserrat**.
- **Rack railway (Cremallera)** — get off at **Monistrol de Montserrat**.

Get off at the wrong one and you're waiting for the next train. Set the station name in your head before you board.

### Cable car or rack railway?

**Cable car:** about 15 minutes, and the views on the way up are extraordinary. It's also a steep, exposed ride in a small cabin — genuinely daunting if you don't like heights, and I say that as someone who was fine with it.

**Rack railway:** about 45 minutes, more gradual, and much easier if the cable car sounds like a bad time. You still get a scenic climb, just a gentler one.

There's no wrong answer. If you have any doubt about heights, take the railway — you're not missing the point of the day.

There are also combined tickets that bundle the funiculars at the top of the mountain. Worth it if you plan to use them; if you're hiking to Sant Jeroni on foot, as we did, you don't need them.

## At the top

### The monastery

Buy the ticket to go inside the basilica. The interior is genuinely stunning, and it's the reason the mountain has had visitors for a thousand years rather than fifty.

The complex holds the **Virgin of Montserrat** — the Black Madonna — and there's usually a queue to see the statue itself. There's also the **Escolania**, one of the oldest boys' choirs in Europe, which performs on a set schedule most days. If you want to hear it, check the timetable before you plan your hike, because it's the one part of the day you can't move.

![Montserrat](/images/spain/articles/montserrat.jpg)

### The Sant Jeroni hike

This is what turns Montserrat from a two-hour stop into a full day, and it's the reason I'd rank it among the best things I did in Spain.

**Sant Jeroni** is the highest point on the massif. From the monastery, the hike is around **two hours up** through dense forest with serious, sustained elevation gain, and about **an hour and a half back down**. It's demanding — not technical, but it doesn't let up — and you'll want reasonable fitness and real shoes.

The views from the summit are worth every bit of it: the whole valley on one side and the rock towers of the massif dropping away on the other. On a clear day you can see a very long way.

**Total time for the hike:** budget around 5 hours including stops at the top.

If that's more than you want, there are shorter marked trails around the monastery — including the walk to **Santa Cova**, the chapel below the complex — that give you the scenery without the climb.

## How to plan the day

The full version, which is what I'd recommend:

**08:00** — Train from Plaça d'Espanya. Go early; this fills up.
**09:00** — Up the mountain by cable car or rack railway.
**09:30** — Monastery and basilica.
**11:00** — Start the Sant Jeroni hike.
**16:00** — Back down at the monastery.
**17:00** — Descend and take the train back to Barcelona.

The short version, without the summit hike, comfortably fits into a half day — monastery, a short trail, and back in Barcelona for the afternoon.

## Practical notes

- **Go early.** The monastery gets very busy from mid-morning, and the queues for the way up build with it.
- **Take water and food.** There are places to eat at the monastery but they're limited and priced for a captive audience. There's nothing at all on the Sant Jeroni trail.
- **Real shoes for the hike.** The path is rocky and steep. Trainers minimum.
- **Check the last train back** before you start hiking. It's the one genuine risk in the day.
- **Dress respectfully for the basilica** — covered shoulders and knees.
- **Weather changes on the mountain.** It's meaningfully cooler and windier at the top than in Barcelona. Take a layer even on a warm day.

## Is it worth a day of your Barcelona trip?

Yes — and I'd go further and say if you have three days or more in Barcelona, this should be one of them. It's the rare day trip that gives you a world-class building and a serious mountain hike within the same few hours, and it's a complete change of scene from the city an hour behind you.

Of everywhere I went in Spain, this was one of only a handful of places I'd go back to without hesitating.
