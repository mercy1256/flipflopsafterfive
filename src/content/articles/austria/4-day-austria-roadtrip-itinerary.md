Four days across Austria by car, from the Zillertal to Salzburg. Two things are worth saying before the itinerary itself.

**The best single thing on this trip is the hike up to the Olpererhütte** — one of the most rewarding alpine trails I've ever done, and it takes half a day.

**And the Großglockner High Alpine Road is worth the toll**, which was €40 when I went. That's a lot of money for a road, and I'd pay it again without thinking about it.

---

## Day 1: Achensee, the Olpererhütte, and Innsbruck

### Achensee

A turquoise alpine lake sometimes called the "Fjord of the Alps", right on the border with Germany. **We swam, and it was beautiful — but we were there about an hour in total.**

That's the useful framing: Achensee is a stop, not a destination. Give it an hour, get in the water, and move on. The rest of the day is the reason you're here.

![Achensee](/images/austria/articles/achensee.jpg)

### Schlegeisspeicher and the Olpererhütte

A reservoir sitting between peaks at the head of the Zillertal, reached by a seasonal toll road, with a car park at the dam. The hike up to the **Olpererhütte** ends at a narrow suspension bridge strung over a ravine with the reservoir directly below — the most photographed spot in the valley, and deservedly.

**Budget two hours up, not one.** An hour is the figure you'll see quoted everywhere; at an unhurried pace it took us closer to two.

![Schlegeisspeicher](/images/austria/articles/schlegeisspeicher.jpg)

I've written this hike up properly, including the timing trick that got us the bridge without a queue: **[hiking to the Olpererhütte](/blog/experiences/hiking/olpererhutte-hiking)**.

### Innsbruck

We stayed the night here. The Old Town and the Golden Roof are a pleasant hour's wander, and it's well placed for the next morning — but on a four-day trip this is a bed and a dinner rather than a day.

![Innsbruck](/images/austria/articles/innsbruck.jpg)

## Day 2: The Großglockner High Alpine Road

The whole day, and the drive most people build an Austrian road trip around.

### What the toll buys you

**The toll was €40 when I went** — worth checking the current price, since it changes. **It was more than worth it.**

**Driven without stopping, the road takes roughly two hours.** Do not drive it without stopping. That figure is only useful as the number to plan *around*: it means you can spend a full day on the road and still be somewhere for dinner.

![Großglockner Hochalpenstraße](/images/austria/articles/grossglockner-hochalpenstrasse.jpg)

### The best stop wasn't the famous one

This is the thing I'd most want you to take from this article.

Everyone drives to **Kaiser-Franz-Josefs-Höhe**, the big visitor centre at the end of the spur, and walks down toward the glacier. We did too. It was nice — but **the glacier is visibly thinning**, and honestly, **other places along the road were far more breathtaking.**

**The best spot we found was an unnamed waterfall beside the road.** We spotted it while driving, found a pull-off nearby, and walked back to it. The surroundings were epic, and it isn't on anyone's list.

So: don't treat this road as a drive to a destination. **The pull-offs are the attraction.** Stop when something looks good, including when it doesn't have a name or a car park. That's where the day actually is.

### Zell am See

We stayed the night here, and I'll be blunt about it: **it has a lake, but you'll see better views at almost any random lake in Austria.** It's a perfectly good place to sleep after the Großglockner and nothing more. Don't build time into your itinerary for it.

## Day 3: Dachstein Ice Caves and Hallstatt

### Dachstein Ice Caves

**Worth it.** You take a cable car up, and the guided route through the caves is genuinely beautiful inside — frozen waterfalls and lit ice formations. On a four-day trip it earns its half-day.

![Dachstein Ice Caves](/images/austria/articles/dachstein-ice-caves.jpg)

### Hallstatt — and why the car matters

**Hallstatt was overrun.** That's not a reason to skip it. It is genuinely that beautiful, and **I still stop by whenever I'm anywhere near it.**

But here's the part most itineraries miss. **Because we had the car, we could stay late** — and once the coaches have pulled out, the village empties. **Hallstatt at night is beautiful**, and almost nobody who day-trips in ever sees it.

![Hallstatt](/images/austria/articles/hallstatt.jpg)

**We slept in Gosau**, a short drive away, rather than in Hallstatt itself. That's worth knowing if Hallstatt's own accommodation is full or priced like it usually is — you don't need to stay in the village to get the evening version of it.

## Day 4: Salzburg

Salzburg is **a good day trip** to finish on.

**Hohensalzburg Fortress** dominates the city — one of the largest medieval castles in Europe. **We took the funicular up** rather than walking, which is the easy call if you've spent three days in hiking boots already.

Below it, **Salzburg Cathedral** and its baroque interior, and **Mozart's birthplace** on Getreidegasse.

---

## Worth knowing

- **The Olpererhütte is the highlight.** If you cut one thing from this itinerary, don't cut this.
- **Allow two hours for that hike**, not the hour that gets quoted.
- **The Großglockner toll was €40** and it's worth it — but only if you stop constantly. Driving it straight through in two hours wastes the money.
- **The unnamed pull-offs beat Kaiser-Franz-Josefs-Höhe.** The glacier is thinning and the road itself is the attraction.
- **Achensee needs an hour**, not an afternoon.
- **Zell am See is a bed, not a stop.**
- **Stay near Hallstatt, not in it** — Gosau worked well — and stay into the evening. The village at night is the version worth having.
- **You want a car for this.** Every good decision on this trip depended on having one.
